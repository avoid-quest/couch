# Positioning

How Couch explains itself, and why. The [vision](vision.md) says where it's
going; [voice](voice.md) says how to write it; [claims](claims.md) says what
we may say today.

## The gap

Couch's public line was *"Streams and radio on one screen. Every phone is a
remote."* That was true and it undersells the product twice over:

- **A phone isn't only a remote.** On the latest preview a phone or laptop can
  also be a screen or a speaker for the Couch, and can share its own screen or
  sound. "Remote" describes the least interesting thing it does.
- **"One screen" is the wrong unit.** Couch already plays one thing on several
  devices, its sound in sync, and its long-term ambition is a room full of
  people listening to one live source on their own devices. The story has to
  hold the living room, a game night and a gig without becoming a feature list.

Phones-as-remotes is also a crowded category (Chromecast, AirPlay, Plex,
Spotify Connect). Couch's difference is not the remote; it's that the people in
the room, with whatever devices they brought, all take part.

## Three directions

**A. The living-room player.** *"Watch and listen together. Every phone is a
remote and a speaker."* Concrete and true today, approachable. But it's a
bigger version of the old line: sharing and live sound read as add-ons, and
it competes on the category's terms.

**B. A sound system made of the phones in the room.** *"Plug in. Everyone
hears it on their phone."* Distinctive and memorable, and it is where the most
ambition lives. But it isn't available yet, it makes the everyday product look
like a side feature, and it invites exactly the latency and crowd-size claims
we can't support.

**C. Everyone joins in. (Recommended.)** Couch turns the devices people already
have into one shared player. Whoever is in the room joins from their phone or
laptop; each device can choose what plays, play it, or share into it. The
living room is the first room; game night and the gig are bigger ones.

C wins because it's true at today's scale and grows without changing shape:
the concert is the same promise, with more people and harder sound. It keeps
the name working (a couch is where people gather around something playing)
and it gives the three experiences one grammar. Its risk is abstraction:
"join in" says little on its own. So C must always be carried by a concrete
scene, and the first sentence a newcomer reads must still say *media player*.

## The recommended story

**One sentence.** Couch is a media player that everyone around it shares:
run it on one computer, join from phones and laptops with a QR code, and
together choose what plays and which devices play it.

**Short story.** Most players have one owner and one screen. Couch has everyone who's there.
Open it on the computer by the TV and everyone who scans the code can search,
queue and skip, so the next video is whoever found it first. The same phones
that choose can also play: the TV gets the picture, the kitchen laptop gets the
sound, and they keep time with each other. A device can send what it's showing
too, so a game on someone's laptop lands on the big screen. Where Couch is
going: a mixer plugged into the Couch computer, and everyone at the gig
listening on their own phone or headphones, close to live and in step.

**Central promise.** *Whoever's here can join in, with what they already
have.* No accounts, no app to install on phones, no special speakers, and
nothing to pay: Couch is free and open source.

**Proof.**

- Join by scanning a QR code or typing six digits, in the phone's browser.
- Everyone paired can search, play, queue and control; an admin looks after
  the Couch.
- **Where it plays** puts picture and sound on any mix of the devices that
  allow it; sound plays in sync across them.
- **Share** sends a screen, a window, a computer's sound or an audio input to
  other devices, fast.
- It runs on your own network, from a Mac, Windows or Linux computer, or as a
  dedicated Debian TV.

**Three ways to join in.** Every public explanation keeps these apart:

| | What it means | Today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Choose together** | Shared control: everyone can find, queue and steer what plays | Available |
| **Share live** | A device sends its screen or sound to others, as fast as possible; each plays it as soon as it arrives | Available |
| **Play in sync** | Several devices play the same sound at the same moment, like one system | Available for media and, a few seconds behind, for shared sources. Close-to-live sync is the live sound work |

**Today and tomorrow.** Today you can already share a sound card's input from
the Couch computer to other devices, and choose between close to live (each phone on
its own clock) and in step (a few seconds behind). Live sound is the work to
have both at once, with good sound and steady recovery. That one sentence is
how the website, README and vision should relate the present to the future:
the ambition is a promise about *quality*, not a new product.

## The headline is open

The owner doesn't want "room" as the frame, and none of these was right:
"A media player everyone shares", "Play it together", "Everyone joins in",
"One player, everyone's devices". Each is accurate but names one part of Couch
(sharing, togetherness, joining, devices). The headline we want unites them.
"A media player everyone shares" is a placeholder in the README and
descriptions until then. The concept, *everyone joins in*, stands.

## Name and family

Keep **Couch**. It names the gathering, not the furniture: the place people sit
together around something playing. The app already says "a Couch" for one
host and the devices joined to it ("Another Couch", "the Couch's name"), so a
gig can be a very big Couch. **avoid.quest** is the larger scope (avoid as a quest) and Couch is one of
its projects, as the address says. Write the site's name as
`couch.avoid.quest`, the product as **Couch**, and say "a project of
avoid.quest" once per surface, linked; don't explain the name. avoid radio,
built in, is the family's shared thread. Couch is free and open source, and
welcomes forks and contributions: say so plainly, never as a sales point.
