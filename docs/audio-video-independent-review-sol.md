**Independent architecture opinion — 24 October 2026**

Main and this workspace both match `59ac6cd6a510ca96bf60dfc9a3628cc635be22e1`. This is source/document review, including material dirty experiment files and stopped-thread records. No Couch/service or benchmark was run, no hardware was used, and no other reviewer was read or contacted. “Observed” below means inspected code or retained evidence; recommendations are proposals.

**My recommendation is two playback families:** ordinary Direct through the platform player, and scheduled playback through one audio delivery module with a common presentation timeline. Captures can use scheduled playback with one receiver. Independent users retain independent Playbacks and queues. I found no materially simpler credible alternative. WebRTC is an optional additional path only under the owner's already-settled latency-benefit condition; its advantage over the proposed implementation remains unknown.

I support that target, but would not approve the draft as a complete implementation architecture. Its important unresolved work is concentrated in the following five findings.

1. **Sharing scheduled audio is sensible; “different timing budgets” understates the behavioral differences.**

Observed: current Sendspin starts with a one-second lead, uses 60 ms Opus/100 ms PCM chunks and buffers ahead. When the source falls behind, it moves the presentation base. A joining receiver requiring another wire format can restart the entire run ([constants](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-73a0194a/src/server/sendspin.ts:29), [join](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-73a0194a/src/server/sendspin.ts:170), [timeline adjustment](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-73a0194a/src/server/sendspin.ts:291)). These are consequential differences from fixed-deadline live playback, beyond changing buffer sizes.

Proposed seam: source adapters supply timestamped audio into shared format preparation, fanout and receiver scheduling. Seekable sources decode ahead; live sources keep an immutable capture-to-presentation mapping. Late joins wait for a viable future packet; incompatible formats are refused before joining; one receiver's reconnect or queue failure cannot retime healthy outputs. Source discontinuities start a new generation. Relaxed buffering remains generous. Receiver admission, bounded queues and rejoining need production behavior, not just server-side send checks.

Use Opus as the initial qualified live format. Do not automatically carry relaxed PCM compatibility into the live promise: the pinned player deliberately excludes Firefox Opus, and browser/output support must be explicit ([published package](https://registry.npmjs.org/@sendspin/sendspin-js/-/sendspin-js-5.0.0.tgz), `dist/core/codec-support.js:33`). One scheduling model is credible; an unchanged Sendspin wrapper is insufficient.

2. **MSE is a reasonable small candidate, not an established synchronized-live video solution.**

Observed: Couch's follower updates a TimingObject every 250 ms; the renderer maps HLS timestamps into its audio timeline ([follower](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-73a0194a/src/client/session/picture-clock.ts:7), [mapping](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-73a0194a/src/client/session/renderer.ts:201)). Neither the refresh interval nor accurate media position proves physical screen alignment. Fewer screens reduce traffic and decoding load; they do not solve presentation timing.

Short timestamped MSE fragments can retain platform decoding without another encode. But they still require capture/encoding, initialization/keyframe delivery, packaging, timestamp mapping, bounded append queues and recovery. MSE's [timestamp offsets and live seekable ranges](https://w3c.github.io/media-source/) manage a media timeline, not wall-clock display deadlines. Native-HLS fallback currently lacks the mapping used by the follower; that cannot silently qualify for synchronized live.

I would keep MSE/follower reuse as the first candidate and keep the renderer choice open. A WebCodecs frame scheduler earns its larger implementation only if explicit frame selection/drop control is necessary; [Chrome's example](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/best-practices/webcodecs#decoding) leaves scheduling/rendering to the application. It still cannot guarantee physical scanout. Additional buffering buys readiness, not automatically tighter skew.

Define three separate engineering acceptance measures: speaker-to-speaker alignment, screen-to-screen alignment, and local lip sync. Map video timestamps through the same source-time relationship as normalized audio, including on video-only sources without synthetic audio. Propose practical acceptance criteria from the use cases; do not make the owner invent milliseconds or infer a gaming requirement from the concert target.

3. **“Encode once” needs an explicit capture and clock owner.**

Observed: the successful native experiment uses ALSA sample-position/status observations and stateful source resampling. Current product capture instead obtains browser MediaStreams; its constraints do not guarantee stereo ([source clock](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/experiment-stereo-clock-discipline/src/server/live-stereo-clock.ts:16), [browser acquisition](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-73a0194a/src/client/session/live.ts:253)). The native result does not establish a browser ADC timestamp or browser A/V timestamp relationship.

My simplest initial design would upload timestamped PCM to Core, normalize its source clock there, then encode Opus once. Video arrives encoded once with preserved source timestamps. This centralizes audio processing but explicitly costs a raw-audio uplink. Placing normalization/encoding at the publisher is another viable implementation, not a free substitution. Avoid encoding audio before Core normalization if that merely forces a decode/re-encode later.

Source normalization and each receiver's output-clock correction remain separate responsibilities. The [Sendspin source specification](https://github.com/Sendspin/spec/blob/aaa18a4b0b34fc9135ce8fdf3c3229018c633aab/README.md#client--server-source-audio) explicitly distinguishes capture timestamps from player deadlines. Prefer a narrow authenticated Couch ingestion adapter initially: the [pinned server README](https://registry.npmjs.org/@sonn-audio/node-sendspin/-/node-sendspin-0.6.2.tgz) says encrypted `source@v1` lacks required pairing support. A protocol upgrade is not inherently necessary.

4. **Preserve useful behavior while removing conversions, rather than preserving every mechanism.**

Keep concurrent queues, explicit Play on isolation, exclusive Move here, shared controls and device consent ([ownership](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-73a0194a/src/server/hub.ts:804)). Each device serves one Playback; ordinary live Play normally joins the source's existing Playback. Preserve per-device volume/mute, mix attenuation, supported delay controls, newest-tab ownership, reconnect cleanup and source/load generations. Content audio delay and speaker Sync delay are different controls; do not claim unsupported adapters already honor both. Stop listening must leave the publisher and other receivers alive; publisher departure must end its source ([lifetime](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-73a0194a/src/server/live-input.ts:130)).

Keep yt-dlp/provider resolution and protected byte relays: resolved public media uses the relay even when direct fetching works, and relaying is not transcoding ([policy](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-73a0194a/src/server/pass-through.ts:145)). Keep upstream HLS/MPEG-TS playback. Keep mpv as a narrow host codec backend, AirPlay as an audio input, and native/Bluetooth routing as sink control; removing them loses those capabilities, without simplifying the shared timing problem. None qualifies an otherwise unsupported live output. AirPlay's current 250 ms ingress prebuffer makes it relaxed input ([buffer](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-73a0194a/src/server/airplay-audio.ts:10)).

Combine captured and AirPlay audio ingestion with scheduled delivery. Remove generated AAC/HLS as their audio intermediate, duplicated conversions and synthetic clock audio. Eventually remove the recorder/transcode pipeline once its compatibility obligation is settled. Keep neither an SFU nor WebTransport merely for 50 audio receivers. TURN/STUN and peer signaling remain only with a justified WebRTC path. The present “Behind” fallback is a separate compatibility capability; deleting it needs an explicit scope decision, and retaining it must not become a synchronized-live fallback.

5. **The experiments supply mechanisms, not a qualified 50-receiver engine. The cost explanation is mostly accurate.**

Useful candidates are source-rate estimation, stereo resampling, fixed deadlines, generation/capacity guards and the isolated SDK clock arithmetic fix. The lab sender hardcodes group/volume, supports PCM only and has no product routing/video contract ([sender](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/experiment-stereo-clock-discipline/src/server/live-stereo.ts:18)); its build transform is not a shipping SDK fix. Fresh-session preparation does not establish arbitrary-idle recovery. Retain evidence/calibration/evaluator repairs as harness work. Separately consider speaker-only joins and unchanged-volume suppression; peer-negotiation improvements matter only if that transport survives. Reject duplicated mono, global desktop buffer tuning and wholesale lab integration as the phone solution.

Spot-checked retained qualifications confirm the 350 ms checkpoint and R5 100/95 ms longs; R5 90 ms has shorts only and its long never acquired ([stop record](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/experiment-stereo-clock-discipline/.scratch/latency-investigation/r5-operations/STOPPED.md)). Earlier revisions include lower passing longs and later failures. These results are one wired stereo receiver, PCM, without synchronized video. Send-ahead, subsequent sharing/helper lifecycle and compressor improvements are already in main; neither experimental sender is integrated ([indexed provenance](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-73a0194a/docs/audio-video-experiment-evidence.md:50)). Corrected older 50-browser runs used actual decoders with synthetic input/virtual outputs, concealed samples and failed recovery gates ([original report](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-6c0b21ea/docs/live-audio-latency-results.md:150)). They cannot be combined with the wired experiment into physical-50 qualification.

The HTML's 6.4 Mbit/s Opus and 76.8 Mbit/s PCM calculations for 50 are correct. Its 128 kbit/s Opus is illustrative; current main requests 96 kbit/s. The 3E/3D → 1E/1D audio comparison is legitimate stage counting, not measured CPU/latency improvement. Tighten its claim that relaxed/live differ only in timing budget, and its generic relaxed badge mixing URL and raw-capture decode counts ([cases](/home/avoid/.t3/worktrees/couch/t3-73a0194a/docs/audio-video-costs.html:103)). Encode-once still leaves receiver decoding, per-client encryption/transmission, Wi-Fi airtime, TCP stalls and join bursts. Phone background operation, output changes, Opus latency and physical skew remain unqualified; video also needs a count/quality envelope. Re-admit after output-route changes and qualify locked-screen audio separately from foreground video. Media Session controls do not establish background timing; browser [output-latency estimates](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.1/#dom-audiocontext-outputlatency) do not qualify physical alignment.

Only two consequential owner choices remain here: whether ordinary sharing may lose its old delayed compatibility fallback, and the initial video-subset count/quality to support. The architecture can be agreed without another performance campaign; 100 ms remains the concert goal, with support claims limited by existing evidence.
